Places to look for stories, characters, creatures, and visual design. **These lists were written with ChatGPT**, from the **Inspiration** tab of my [inspiration doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/17gIm47t000_5_PasGklTHhs3f8hmvAtOcnp2sxa1ZIo/edit).

## Story

  - **Folklore and myth collections:** Japanese yokai tales, Celtic and Norse legends, West African Anansi stories, Chinese Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio, and the Brothers Grimm. Project Gutenberg has many of these free. Retelling an old tale in a new setting is a classic way to get a strong drama structure.

  - **Production**: Built using generative tools like Seedance 2.5 for video generation, ElevenLabs for voiceovers, and Suno for dramatic background music.

  - **Award-winning shorts:** Short of the Week, Vimeo Staff Picks, Oscar-shortlisted animated shorts, and Annecy and Sundance short programs. Watch for how they tell a full emotional arc in 3-8 minutes.

  - **Prompt and idea banks:** r/WritingPrompts, r/shortstories, and "what if" questions ("what if a painter's brushstrokes came alive when she grieves?").

  - **Personal emotional truths:** Drama tends to land when it's about something small and universal, like a lost bond, a promise, an inheritance, or leaving home. The fantasy elements then express the emotion.

## Characters

  - **Character-heavy films to study:** Kubo and the Two Strings, Song of the Sea, The Tale of the Princess Kaguya, Wolfwalkers, and Spirited Away.
  - **Real-life observation:** old photographs, street portraits, and historical archives. The Library of Congress and the Met's Open Access collection are free and huge.

## Pets, companions, and fantasy creatures

  - **Bestiaries**: medieval bestiaries, the D&D Monster Manual, Japanese Hyakki Yagyō scrolls (parades of spirits and creatures), and mythical creature encyclopedias.
  - **Companion-creature models**: Momo in Avatar, the fox-squirrel Kirara in Nausicaä, Ponyo, and the spirit animals in Princess Mononoke.
  - **Mash-up approach**: combine a real animal with a symbolic trait, like a crane made of paper that remembers things, or a fox whose tails are lit lanterns.

## Design and look
  - **Visual reference hubs**: ArtStation, Behance, Pinterest mood boards, and Film-Grab or ShotDeck for real film stills.
  - **Palette tools**: Coolors and Adobe Color. Pull palettes from a favorite painting or film frame.
  - **Silhouette test**: if the creature or character is recognizable as a flat black shape, the design is strong.
  - **Museum open-access collections**: the Met, Rijksmuseum, and Wikimedia Commons for costumes, patterns, and architecture.
