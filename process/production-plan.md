My plan for getting from a finished plot to the generated clips and this site, from the **to-do** tab of my [inspiration doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/17gIm47t000_5_PasGklTHhs3f8hmvAtOcnp2sxa1ZIo/edit).

| Priority | Task | What you need finished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Finish the plot using the two office-hours additions** | Enough clarity to identify the necessary characters, scenes, and central dramatic moments. |
| **2** | **Think through how the clips will form one trailer** | A rough sequence with dialogue/voiceover, transitions, pacing, and the final hook. This determines what you actually need to generate. |
| **3** | **Fix the mascot’s style** | An approved design that fits the drama and looks coherent beside the man. |
| **4** | **Make the man’s character sheet** | Consistent references for your main character. |
| **5** | **Make the Boston reference sheet** | Real locations and generated settings selected for the scenes you need. |
| **6** | **Make sheets for the other needed characters** | Start with characters essential to the trailer’s strongest moments. |
| **7** | **Plan character voices** | Voice choices and a decision about visible dialogue versus voiceover for each scene. |
| **8** | **Prepare prompts and prioritize tomorrow’s generations** | Ready-to-use shot prompts and references, ordered by importance, with still-image fallbacks. |
| **9** | **Make the workflow/decision chart** | Clear next steps for successful clips, specific failure types, retries, and low credits. |
| **10** | **Finalize and submit the context package** | Package your plot, references, creative decisions, and production plan for both submissions. |
